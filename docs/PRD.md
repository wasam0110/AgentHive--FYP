# Agent Hive
## Product Requirements Document

**Version:** 2.0 | **Date:** 25 October 2026 | **Status:** Implementation baseline, not a claim of completed functionality

**Project:** Autonomous Multi-Agent Customer Operations System
**Context:** Final Year Project, DHA Suffa University, AI Batch 2023
**Team:** Wasam, Arif, Asad, Shizza

---

## 1. The idea in plain words

Agent Hive is an independent, configurable multi-agent customer-operations platform. It is not tied to an industry or named client. Businesses adapt a common core through validated configuration, their own knowledge sources and catalogs, and allowlisted integration adapters.

For example, “I need help with my earlier request, and I would like a quote and an appointment” may invoke support, sales and scheduling together. Workers propose; backend code validates permissions, prices, consent, approval and availability before any write.

Business independence does not mean unrestricted autonomy or immediate support for every possible operation. Existing capabilities can be configured without core edits; new business actions require implemented, tested adapters. Unsupported actions must be declined or referred for manual handling.

Development uses synthetic data. One configured business runs per deployment; commercial multi-tenant hosting is future scope. Switching test profiles uses separate isolated test data, never silently reassigns existing customers or conversations.

## 2. Problem and objectives

Small businesses may receive customer messages involving multiple departments. A basic FAQ chatbot cannot reliably coordinate support, sales, scheduling, and approvals in the same conversation.

Agent Hive must:

1. Identify and track multiple requests within one conversation.
2. Use stored business information and support documents rather than inventing facts.
3. Coordinate specialized workers, running independent work in parallel.
4. Require human approval and customer consent where applicable.
5. Execute permitted changes once, with a durable audit trail.
6. Remain usable after a stream disconnect or backend restart.
7. Measure whether specialization and parallel execution improve outcomes compared with a single agent.

Success does not require multi-agent execution to win every comparison. Honest results, including higher costs or slower simple requests, are valid research outcomes.

## 3. Scope and release boundaries

### Required full FYP release (by FYP-II)

- A reusable core, one configured business per deployment, and two synthetic configuration fixtures that verify adaptation without core-code edits.
- Intake, supervisor, support, sales, and scheduling roles.
- English, Roman Urdu, and Urdu-script interaction for the defined business scenarios, including mixed English/Roman Urdu messages.
- Support RAG over a small, reviewed knowledge base.
- Database-backed proposals, validation, customer confirmation, approval, and execution.
- Five dashboard screens, authentication, role-based permissions, and redacted audit traces.
- Durable workflows, bounded retries, duplicate-execution prevention, and REST recovery without WebSockets.
- Three research configurations, development cases, locked holdout cases, and reproducible reporting.
- Docker-based local setup, automated tests, documentation, and a live demonstration.

### Optional after the required release passes

- WhatsApp or notification integration.
- Local-model provider adapter and offline demonstration mode.
- Additional business policies and more supported intents.
- A second isolated tenant as a separate extension, with explicit isolation tests.

### Outside the FYP baseline

- Commercial white-label SaaS, platform subscription billing, self-service tenant onboarding, or cross-business administration.
- Live payment processing, actual refunds, production account cancellation, or industry-specific equipment control.
- Voice calling, autonomous learning from corrections, unrestricted web browsing, arbitrary code execution, or unrestricted SQL generated by the model.
- General-purpose multilingual support outside the defined scenarios.

Business configuration, catalog, knowledge sources, terminology and capability selection are required features. Configurability must not be described as secure multi-tenancy. Future tenant support requires isolation across storage, retrieval, APIs, approvals, caches, and streams.

## 4. Users and permissions

| User | Allowed work |
|---|---|
| Customer | Send messages, view their own conversation, choose a proposal or appointment, confirm or decline their own actions. |
| Operator | Monitor conversations, investigate traces, draft a custom response, and request manual handling. Cannot approve restricted actions. |
| Approver/admin | Operator capabilities plus approve or reject sensitive actions within configured authority. |
| Evaluator | View benchmark results and permitted redacted traces; cannot modify customer accounts. |

For the FYP, the Chat Simulator uses an authenticated staff session to select a seeded customer. The backend validates that simulation capability and binds the conversation to the selected customer. This is explicitly a simulation, not proof of customer authentication. A real customer channel must establish identity server-side before exposing account data or accepting account changes.

Permissions are enforced by backend endpoints and tool adapters, not by hiding buttons. Customer IDs and future tenant IDs supplied in a message are untrusted.

## 5. Supported business actions

| Intent/action | Expected behavior | Execution boundary |
|---|---|---|
| FAQ or troubleshooting | Retrieve relevant guidance and provide cited steps. | Read-only; no approval. |
| Support complaint | Check customer context, known service status records, and ticket history; explain findings and create a ticket when appropriate. | Ticket creation permitted under policy; never claim a reported issue is fixed without evidence. |
| Catalog enquiry | Return eligible catalog items and database prices. | Read-only; no approval. |
| Service request | Calculate an explicit quote, collect acceptance, request admin approval, then record the simulated service request. | Customer consent plus admin approval required in the baseline. |
| Resource appointment | Propose available slots; customer chooses one; recheck and book. | Customer consent required; admin approval also required for a fee or linked billing change. |
| Discount request | Prepare a policy-valid proposal or explain why it is unavailable. | Customer consent and admin approval; configured limit is a hard cap for this release. |
| Refund/cancellation request | Gather request details and create an approval/manual-resolution case. | No real financial refund or service cancellation executor in the baseline. After approval, report “approved for manual processing,” not “completed.” |
| Unclear/unsupported request | Ask a focused clarification or route to a human. | No speculative mutation. |

Synthetic fixture policy values: maximum discount 5%; pending admin approval expires after 48 hours; price quotes expire after 30 minutes. These are explicit test-profile values, not universal business rules. Required deployment policies must be supplied and validated; missing values do not silently inherit a sample policy. Appointment suggestions are not reserved unless an explicit hold exists; the baseline uses no temporary holds.

All money calculations use decimal arithmetic and a configured currency. Seeded demo data may use PKR. The quote must show catalog line items, quantities, applicable unit prices, discount, total, currency and validity. Recurring charges are supported only if an explicit pricing adapter implements them; the first adapter handles simple one-time quotes. Proration and taxes are excluded unless explicit rules and data are added; never silently invent them.

## 6. End-to-end workflow

1. Authenticate the caller, bind customer identity, persist the message, and assign conversation/run IDs.
2. Intake extracts intents, relevant entities, language preference, and missing information into a validated schema.
3. The supervisor builds a task dependency plan. Independent support investigation and catalog lookup may run concurrently.
4. Workers use allowed read tools and return structured findings, evidence references, and action proposals.
5. Backend validation checks schema, ownership, permissions, business rules, and current records.
6. If essential information is missing, ask the customer one focused question and persist the waiting state.
7. Present an exact quote or appointment offer and collect explicit customer acceptance where needed.
8. Obtain admin approval for the exact sensitive proposal. The workflow remains durable while waiting.
9. Revalidate current price, eligibility, resource availability, consent, approval, and expiry immediately before execution.
10. Execute eligible actions through a controlled executor, record receipts, and return a coherent customer response.

The default order is customer acceptance followed by admin approval. Both must refer to the same proposal version. A changed price, slot, or action invalidates old consent and approval. If a quote expires while approval is pending, issue a fresh quote and obtain fresh confirmation and approval.

### Dependency and conflict rules

- Support investigation and catalog lookup can run together.
- Scheduling that depends on service eligibility waits for the sales result.
- An unresolved complaint does not automatically block a service request; eligibility and business rules decide.
- Conflicting service request and cancellation intents require clarification; do not execute both.
- Live business records determine prices and availability; documents cannot override them.
- Worker disagreement triggers re-query, clarification, or escalation; never resolve it by blending incompatible facts.
- Successful independent work may remain complete when another intent fails. Show each outcome clearly.
- A coupled service request and linked booking must be committed together in one database transaction for the baseline. If either validation fails, neither record change is applied. External integrations will require a separately designed compensation process.

## 7. Agent and backend responsibilities

| Component | Responsibilities | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Extract multiple intents and entities, identify missing facts and language. | Treat customer claims as verified account data. |
| Supervisor | Build dependency plan, select workers, reconcile results, request clarification, compose response from verified outcomes. | Bypass policy or declare success without an execution receipt. |
| Support worker | Read service status records, ticket history, and RAG guidance; propose ticket or escalation. | Invent resolution timelines or directly change service requests. |
| Sales worker | Retrieve catalog items and eligibility; request deterministic quote calculation; propose an offer. | Invent prices or perform arithmetic as the authoritative price calculation. |
| Scheduling worker | Retrieve availability and propose suitable slots. | Confirm a reservation before the booking executor succeeds. |
| Policy gate | Deterministic checks returning allow, deny, requires_customer_confirmation, requires_admin_approval, or needs_revalidation. | Delegate final authorization to an LLM. |
| Executor | Perform allowlisted writes after final checks, enforce idempotency, store receipts. | Accept arbitrary SQL or tool names from model output. |
| Trace redactor | Remove secrets and unnecessary sensitive data before storing/displaying diagnostics. | Be the sole security boundary or an LLM-only sanitizer. |

Workers use typed tools with minimal permissions. Write access resides in the executor. A model-provided confidence score can inform diagnostics but cannot grant authorization or substitute for evidence.

The supervisor may use an LLM for planning and wording; legal graph transitions, allowed actions, budgets, and approval prerequisites remain deterministic code.

## 9. Workflow state, approval, and recovery

Persist both conversation status and per-task/per-action status. One task can wait while another succeeds.

Conversation statuses: queued, running, waiting_customer, waiting_admin, partially_completed, completed, failed, cancelled. A completed conversation may include a rejected request if all intents have a documented final resolution; it must not imply every requested action was performed.

Action statuses: proposed, awaiting_customer, awaiting_admin, ready, executing, succeeded, rejected, expired, failed, cancelled. Read-only actions may skip confirmation/approval states. Recovery of an executing action first checks its canonical receipt and idempotency key before retrying.

Persist customer and admin decisions separately. Approval records include proposal ID/version/hash, approver identity and role, policy version, decision, reason, and timestamp. Decisions are append-only audit events. Concurrent approve/reject calls must use an atomic state transition so only one wins.

Every mutating request has an idempotency key scoped to its actor and operation. Reuse with the same payload returns the original result; reuse with a different payload returns a conflict. Database constraints and transactions enforce uniqueness, not just an in-memory flag.

Use durable graph checkpoints in PostgreSQL and a durable pending-job mechanism. A backend worker resumes eligible runs after restart. An in-process background coroutine alone is insufficient for recovery. Record business mutation and execution receipt transactionally; publish dashboard events only after commit.

Default budgets: two retries for transient model/tool failures with backoff, one structured-output repair attempt, and a finite graph-step limit (initially 12). Then return a controlled failure or escalate. Never retry a write blindly after a timeout. A model service disruption must not prevent admins from viewing stored records.

## 10. Dashboard requirements

### Screen 1: Operations Overview

Show active conversations, status distribution, pending approval count and oldest age, recent failures, and service health. Show today's resolved-intent rate versus a seven-day average with denominators and timestamps. Refresh every 30 seconds. Display “not enough data” instead of fabricated trends.

### Screen 2: Approval Center

Show exact proposal, customer context, evidence, applicable policy, consent status, quote expiry, and latest validation timestamp. Approve/reject requires an authorized role; use a confirmation dialog for approval and a reason for rejection. A custom response is a message or handoff, not an approval. Show stale proposals and require refresh/reconfirmation. Poll every five seconds while active; conversation-specific events may accelerate refresh but cannot replace queue-wide updates.

### Screen 3: Workflow Inspector

Show a simple timeline with agent/task, status, duration, and outcome. Expand for redacted structured input/output, tool calls, evidence, validation, and execution receipts. Show customer consent and admin approval inline. Distinguish step errors, retries, and terminal run failures. Do not expose hidden model reasoning. Provide labels/icons as well as colors.

### Screen 4: Evaluation and Analytics

Show saved benchmark runs, A/B/C results, per-category and per-language results, latency, cost, validation failures, escalation rate, and RAG faithfulness where available. Benchmark results are static after a run; do not poll them every 30 seconds. Operational analytics may refresh separately. Expensive benchmark execution occurs through an authorized CLI initially, not an unrestricted dashboard button.

### Screen 5: Demo Chat Simulator

Select a seeded customer, scenario, and response language; send messages and confirmations; show task progress and waiting reasons; link to the inspector and approval item. Clearly label simulated account changes. Load development/demo scenarios only, never locked holdout cases. Accept real typed messages so the demonstration is not a fixed script.

### Shared UX rules

- REST data is authoritative; WebSockets are optional notifications.
- On connection/reconnection, buffer notifications, fetch current state, then apply only newer sequenced events. Deduplicate by event ID and refetch on gaps.
- The same reconciliation is required on initial connection because a run may begin before the socket attaches.
- Keep existing data visible during reconnects; show loading, empty, failure, and retry states.
- Do not close on every generic error. Close after terminal run events; recoverable step errors remain visible while processing continues.
- Use React Query for server data and Zustand only for local UI selections.
- Use accessible controls, keyboard navigation, readable contrast, responsive layouts, and CSS variables for status colors.
- Urdu content needs suitable glyph coverage, right-to-left layout, and sensible handling of mixed-direction numbers and plan names. A Latin-only font configuration is insufficient.
- Display appointments in Asia/Karachi for the demo and store timestamps in UTC with explicit timezone conversion.

## 11. RAG and business data

Start with a small reviewed set of business support articles (start with 8–12 synthetic documents; expand only when needed) covering troubleshooting, service status records, service requests, and booking policies. Each document/chunk records its source, version, language, review date, and audience. Retrieval must respect authorized business/customer context; account-specific records belong in typed database tools.

Record retrieved chunk IDs with each support answer. Retrieved text and customer messages are untrusted content: instructions embedded in them cannot grant access or override system policies. If evidence is missing, contradictory, or irrelevant, ask for clarification or escalate. The response must distinguish known status information from general troubleshooting suggestions.

Catalog prices, customer eligibility, slots, and execution status come from authoritative database queries. RAG is not the source of truth for these changing values. Rebuild embeddings through an explicit maintenance command when articles change; keep ingestion versioned and reproducible.

## 12. Technical architecture and implementation decisions

| Layer | Baseline choice | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Backend | Python, FastAPI, Pydantic | APIs, schemas, validation, authentication. |
| Orchestration | LangGraph | Task dependencies, worker execution, pause/resume. |
| Primary storage | PostgreSQL, SQLAlchemy, Alembic | Business records, audit events, durable jobs/checkpoints. |
| Retrieval | ChromaDB with persistent storage | Reviewed support article retrieval. |
| Model access | Provider adapter; one hosted model initially | Keep provider-specific code outside business logic. |
| Frontend | Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind | Operations dashboard. |
| Client data | TanStack Query, Zustand | Server cache and local UI state. |
| Live notifications | WebSockets plus REST recovery | Optional progress updates. |
| Packaging | Docker Compose | Repeatable local environment. |
| Quality | pytest, lint/type checks, k6, CI | Correctness, safety, and load verification. |
| Evaluation | Custom deterministic scoring; RAGAS as supplementary judge | Business outcomes and retrieval-answer assessment. |

Redis is not required for the initial single-deployment release. Add it only when a concrete queue/cache requirement justifies the dependency; durable state remains in PostgreSQL. This intentionally simplifies the original stack.

This PRD does not lock inherited major versions such as Next.js 14 or Node 20. During Phase 0, verify supported mutually compatible versions and model availability, then pin dependencies, container images, embedding model, and provider model ID. Record them in lockfiles and an architecture decision. Do not assume the original stack's version numbers are current.

Logical flow: dashboard/API → durable run → intake → supervisor/dependency graph → workers/read tools → validation and proposal → confirmation/approval → revalidation/executor → receipt and response. Persisted trace events feed both REST and streaming views.

## 14. API contract outline

All routes below are under /api/v1. Final request/response schemas must be documented in OpenAPI before parallel frontend/backend development.

| Method and route | Function |
|---|---|
| POST /auth/login; POST /auth/logout; GET /auth/me | Authenticated session lifecycle. |
| POST /conversations | Create a customer-bound conversation. |
| POST /conversations/{id}/messages | Persist message and enqueue processing; return message/run IDs. |
| GET /conversations/{id} | Current messages and task/action states. |
| GET /conversations/{id}/trace | Paginated persisted trace with sequence cursor. |
| POST /proposals/{id}/confirmation | Accept/decline exact version; require customer/simulation authority. |
| GET /approvals | Authorized paginated queue. |
| POST /approvals/{id}/decision | Approve/reject exact version with reason and idempotency key. |
| POST /conversations/{id}/operator-response | Send custom response/handoff; cannot implicitly approve. |
| GET /overview; GET /health | Operational aggregates and appropriately limited health status. |
| GET /evaluations; GET /evaluations/{id} | Saved benchmark metadata and results. |
| WS /conversations/{id}/events | Authorized notifications with event ID and sequence. |

Use consistent errors: authentication failure, forbidden, not found, validation error, stale/conflicting state, rate limited, and dependency unavailable. No mutation is exposed through GET. Use pagination and bounded message/body sizes. A server-assigned correlation ID links API response, run, trace, and logs.

## 15. Security, privacy, and operational requirements

- Keep model keys and database credentials on the backend, outside source control and frontend bundles. Supply a placeholder-only .env.example.
- Use secure password hashing, expiring authenticated sessions, and backend role checks. For browser sessions, prefer HttpOnly cookies; enable Secure outside localhost and implement CSRF protection for mutations. Protect WebSocket handshakes and validate origin.
- Enforce HTTPS in hosted environments, explicit CORS origins, request limits, and rate limits. Do not publish databases or admin credentials publicly.
- Use typed parameterized queries and allowlisted tools. Reject malformed worker output and unknown action types.
- Treat prompt injection as untrusted input; the enforcement boundary is permissions and policy code.
- Store redacted evidence/action summaries rather than unrestricted model prompts or hidden reasoning. Never log credentials or session tokens.
- Use synthetic customer data for development, evaluation, and public demonstrations. Document that selected message/context data is sent to the configured model provider.
- Initial demo retention: 30 days for conversation/trace content, 90 days for audit/benchmark records; make cleanup explicit and configurable. Preserve necessary non-sensitive evaluation aggregates. These are project defaults, not a compliance certification.
- Persist Docker volumes, test backup/restore of PostgreSQL, and retain knowledge-source versions so the demo can be reconstructed.
- Use bounded concurrency and per-run budgets to prevent runaway tool/model calls. Health failures should produce clear degraded states.

## 16. Research experiment and metrics

### Research questions

1. Does specialization improve successful resolution of multi-intent requests?
2. Does parallel execution reduce automated processing time compared with sequential specialists?
3. What additional token/cost overhead does the multi-agent design introduce?
4. How do outcomes vary by intent category and language?

### Configurations

| Config | Definition |
|---|---|
| A | One general agent handling all supported domains with the same tools and enforcement layer. |
| B | Specialized workers and supervisor, with eligible worker tasks executed sequentially. |
| C | Same specialists, prompts and supervisor as B, with independent worker tasks executed in parallel. |

Keep model/version, tools, business records, retrieval index, policies, consent/approval simulator, case inputs, and available capabilities identical. A requires a generalist prompt; document that difference. Use a common total run budget and report actual calls/tokens. B versus C should differ only in scheduling. Snapshot/reset data between cases/configurations so earlier mutations cannot affect later scores.

### Dataset and procedure

- Build 150 human-reviewed development cases and 30 locked holdout cases. These are targets, not existing assets.
- Include single-intent, multi-intent, ambiguous, conflicting, adversarial, approval, failure, and multilingual examples. Tag categories and language separately; tags can overlap.
- Each case includes initial DB fixture, messages/customer follow-ups, expected intents, permitted actions, expected final records, approval decisions, and forbidden outcomes.
- Keep translations/paraphrases of the same scenario in the same split to limit leakage. Holdout cases never enter prompts, demonstrations, tuning, or the scenario loader.
- Freeze dataset hashes, prompts, code commit, model/settings, retrieval versions, policies, and price assumptions before final evaluation.
- Run each case at least three times per configuration when budget permits; disclose any reduced repetition. Vary configuration order to reduce timing bias.
- Simulate customer/admin responses consistently for automated evaluation. Measure real human waiting separately during the live demo.
- Report raw counts and uncertainty. Thirty holdout cases support a limited FYP evaluation, not broad production claims. If tuned after holdout review, label it development data and create a fresh holdout.

### Metric definitions

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Full-case success | All expected intents correctly resolved, required state changes correct, no forbidden action; divided by all evaluated cases. Correct refusal/escalation counts only where expected. |
| Per-intent resolution | Correctly resolved intents / expected intents; report partial results separately. |
| Intent accuracy | Exact intent-set match plus per-intent precision/recall/F1. |
| Worker routing accuracy | Correct specialist assignment for B/C; mark A not applicable rather than inventing equivalent routing. |
| Business correctness | Exact expected price, eligibility, slot, and database outcome checks. |
| Unauthorized execution | Any mutation missing required permission, consent, approval, or policy validity. Release requirement: zero observed violations in the defined suite. |
| Automated latency | End-to-end processing time excluding customer/admin wait; report median and p95. Include retries. |
| Total elapsed time | Includes waiting; report separately to avoid hiding operational delays. |
| Cost | Model input/output usage and other attributable API cost per attempted case and per successful case; record price date or report tokens when pricing is unknown. |
| Escalation/validation failure | Escalated cases / all cases; failed validations / validation attempts. These are diagnostic, not automatically bad. |
| RAG quality | Evidence relevance and answer faithfulness, using human review plus optional RAGAS judge/version/settings. Not a substitute for business correctness. |

### Proposed engineering targets

- At least 90% full-case success on development cases before freezing; report holdout performance honestly without hiding failures.
- Exact correctness for every committed price and booking operation in the defined tests.
- Zero observed unauthorized writes, duplicate executions, and booking conflicts in safety/integration tests.
- Local message-acceptance API p95 below one second, excluding model processing; target automated turn p95 below 30 seconds on the documented demo setup.
- Test ten concurrent simulated conversations with configured provider limits; document hardware, model, rate limits, latency, and errors. Targets are acceptance goals, not guarantees of provider performance.
- Correct state recovery after stream disconnection and restart at each approval/execution boundary.

## 19. Required demonstration scenarios

1. **Support plus quote:** support and sales run concurrently; customer accepts the quote and a slot; admin approves; linked simulated service request and booking commit; final response distinguishes support findings from completed changes.
2. **Sensitive request rejected or expired:** the system pauses; admin rejects or expiry occurs; no restricted mutation happens; customer receives the actual outcome.
3. **Mixed-language ambiguity and recovery:** a Roman Urdu/English request needs clarification; after clarification a valid task proceeds; disconnect/reconnect or restart demonstrates persisted state without duplicates.

Include a seeded scenario loader for convenience, but permit judges to type new requests. Show the policy gate, evidence, approval audit, and resulting database-backed outcome. ## 22. Business configuration and adapter architecture

The core owns conversations, identity, orchestration, state, RAG infrastructure, policy enforcement, approvals and audit. It must not contain industry-name branches, fixed service names, hardcoded catalog prices or company-specific prompt text.

A versioned business profile defines business ID/display name, timezone, currency, supported languages, terminology, enabled capabilities, knowledge-source references, catalog reference, resource/calendar reference, policy values and adapter IDs. Validate at startup. Invalid profiles or unknown adapter IDs fail clearly. Credentials are separate environment/secret references, never embedded in a profile.

The supported initial capabilities are knowledge_answer, catalog_quote and resource_booking. The built-in sandbox adapter reads synthetic records and records simulated service requests/bookings in the project database. External production actions require a separately authorized integration. Profile data cannot provide executable code, SQL, arbitrary URLs or dynamically imported module paths.

Support, sales and scheduling workers are reusable role implementations. A capability registry built in code determines which tools a profile may enable. Disabled capabilities must be absent from routing/tool access, not merely hidden in the UI. Business terminology customizes labels without changing internal stable action identifiers.

Persistent proposals and receipts include business ID and profile/policy version. Changing a profile must not retroactively change existing approvals. A policy or price change requires revalidation and, if terms change, renewed confirmation and approval. One active profile per deployment is not a claim of secure multi-tenancy.

Required adaptation test: run identical core code against synthetic Profile A (support, quote, booking) and Profile B (support and quote only). Use separate test databases/index namespaces. Verify different branding, currency, catalogs, knowledge answers and policy values; Profile B must reject booking without calling a booking tool. These are compatibility fixtures, not named industries or clients. No core source edit is allowed between runs.

For FYP-I, implement profile validation, capability-based routing, configured retrieval/catalogs/policies and this small two-profile smoke test. Full cross-domain evaluations and additional production adapters remain FYP-II/future scope. See BUSINESS_CONFIGURATION.md for the draft contract; fixture JSON is design input, not executable application configuration until the schema and loader exist.

Version 2.0 supersedes earlier domain-specific assumptions. The scope is business-independent; no first industry or client is assigned. Team ownership and the 15 January internal target remain unchanged.
